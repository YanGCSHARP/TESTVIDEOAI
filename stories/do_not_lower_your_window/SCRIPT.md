# DO NOT LOWER YOUR WINDOW

## Part 1. Narration script (en-US)

**S00 · Cold open** `00:00.0`

There is a gesture every truck driver in America knows. A hand raised beside the cab. Then one finger, pointing down. Lower your window. Daniel Reed had answered that gesture at weigh stations, toll plazas and loading docks for eleven years. It had never cost him more than a few seconds. At two forty-one in the morning, at the eastern edge of Bellwether, Pennsylvania, a man in a protective suit made that gesture again. And for the first time in his life, Daniel kept his hand on the wheel. *[pause 3 s]*

**S01 · An ordinary route** `00:35.5`

Forty minutes earlier, the night had been ordinary. Daniel had a twenty-six-foot box truck full of groceries, a delivery window that closed at five, and a dispatcher who only called when something went wrong. A jackknifed tractor-trailer had shut down the interstate, so the routing app sent him off the highway and through Bellwether, a town he had never had a reason to see. The detour added nineteen minutes. Nothing more. The radio played low. The wipers kept time with it. He had driven through smaller towns. He had driven through worse weather. Nothing on the screen beside the wheel suggested anything ahead of him but a two-lane road, a few closed businesses, and the long dark stretch back to the interstate.

**S02 · The sign** `01:17.8`

The sign stood just past the town limit. Temporary steel legs. A portable lamp clamped to the top. A white panel, the kind road crews use for lane closures. Someone had blacked out the printed message and written over it in thick marker, the letters large and uneven, pressed hard enough to tear the surface. Keep vehicle windows closed. Daniel glanced at the window beside him. Already closed. Beyond the trees, the lights of a processing plant glowed in the mist. A chemical leak, he decided. A road crew with no time to print a proper sign. That was the first explanation of the night. It lasted about a minute. An ambulance came toward him in the other lane, emergency lights turning. Its siren was off. *[pause 2 s]*

**S03 · The only lit building** `02:05.7`

Bellwether Fuel was the only lit building on Main Street. Four pumps under a red-and-white canopy. A convenience store with every light on. A blue sedan at the nearest pump. Daniel pulled in for coffee. Out of habit, he parked at the edge of the lot, nose toward the exit. The sedan's driver door stood open. The window was rolled all the way down, and the seat was dark with rain. The keys were still in the ignition. That was why the chime kept sounding. A soft, patient tone, repeating every second, for no one. He waited for the driver to come back out of the store. Nobody did. The driver was in the restroom, he told himself. It was a reasonable explanation. He would stop using that word soon.

**S04 · Unfinished work** `02:51.1`

Inside, the store smelled of burnt coffee and floor cleaner. A paper cup sat under the dispenser. Coffee had overflowed and was still dripping into the tray, which meant the machine had been started minutes ago, not hours. A carton of canned soup sat in the aisle, half unpacked. Behind the register, a phone lit up, buzzed against the counter, and went dark. Daniel looked toward the stockroom door. Something touched the other side of it. Not a knock. A slow scrape, at the height of a man's shoulder. Then the soft pressure of weight settling against the wood.

**S05 · The handle** `03:26.8`

Under the door, in the strip of light from the stockroom, he could see the shadows of two feet. Standing close. Not moving. Daniel took one step back. His heel hit the soup carton. A can rolled across the floor and stopped against the counter. The scrape stopped. Then the handle began to turn. Slowly, all the way to the end of its travel. Whoever stood behind it kept turning, as if the handle might give a little more. Daniel left without the coffee. In the cab, he locked both doors before he started the engine. In his mirror, the store entrance swung open. He turned onto the road before he could see who walked out. Rule one. A lit building is not an open building.

**S06 · Nothing broken** `04:10.9`

Half a mile on, three cars narrowed Main Street to a single lane. There was no wreck. No broken glass. No airbags. No steam rising from a radiator. One car had rolled to a stop with its front wheels against the curb. Another stood across the center line, its engine still running. A grocery bag had spilled oranges into the gutter. Daniel eased between them at walking speed, and that was when he noticed what the three cars had in common. Every driver's window was rolled all the way down. In the last car, a child's rain jacket hung from the open rear door. He searched the sidewalks for a family. There was nobody to flag him down. Nobody asking for help. Rule two. When nothing is broken, ask why everyone left.

**S07 · No signal** `04:59.4`

He called his dispatcher. The call connected, then dropped before he heard a voice. The second attempt didn't connect at all. One bar of signal. Then none. The route screen still showed the same calm blue line. Three point eight miles to the eastern edge of town, then open highway. He thought about turning around. Behind him were the empty cars, the gas station, and whatever had been standing behind that door. Ahead, Main Street ran straight and lit, with working traffic signals and nothing in the road. Daniel kept going. It wasn't courage. It was the oldest mistake people make in an emergency. Choosing the direction where nothing bad has happened yet.

**S08 · The crosswalk** `05:41.8`

The woman was standing at a crosswalk. No umbrella. No coat. A pale sweater gone dark with rain, and her arms hanging straight at her sides. The walk signal in front of her was lit. She didn't walk. Daniel slowed, expecting her to step out in front of him. She didn't move. He waited three seconds. Then he tapped the horn. Her head turned toward the cab. At the same instant, behind the window of a laundromat across the street, someone else turned. So did a figure under the bus shelter. A figure Daniel had looked straight at and taken for a shadow. None of them raised a hand. All three watched the truck go by.

**S09 · The pattern** `06:23.1`

In the mirror, the woman stepped off the curb. She didn't cross. She walked into the lane the truck had just left, and followed it. Not running. Just walking, steadily, in the direction of the sound. Daniel reached over and turned off the radio. He thought of the ambulance. Lights, but no siren. Somebody in this town had already learned something. At the next corner, two men stood under an apartment awning, both facing the wall. He rolled past them with his foot barely on the pedal. No horn. No radio. Neither man turned. Daniel did not have a word for what was wrong with these people. But he understood the pattern well enough to obey it. Rule three. In a silent town, every sound is an announcement.

**S10 · Vehicle screening** `07:06.7`

At Main and Mill Street, a county health department van sat at an angle across one lane. Its rear doors were open. Daniel slowed almost to a stop. Inside, boxed face shields. Unopened protective suits. A folding chair. A cooler with a biohazard sticker. Taped to the inside of the rear door, in the full glare of his headlights, was a printed checklist. He could read the heading from the cab. Vehicle screening. Below it, three steps. Driver window down. Temperature check. Destination. Beside the checklist hung a contact sheet. A long column of addresses. And next to nearly every one, the same two words in thick black marker. No response. The handwriting looked familiar. Daniel couldn't place it. Not yet.

**S11 · The price of one sound** `07:56.3`

There was room to pass. Barely. Daniel brought the truck within a foot of the van, watching the gap in his right mirror. He never saw the loose panel of construction fencing on the left. The rear corner of the box caught it. Steel dragged across steel, and then the whole panel went down in the street with a long, ringing crash. In a second-floor window above the pharmacy, a silhouette rose into view. Then another, behind the pharmacy's dark glass. Inside the open van, something under the pile of protective suits shifted. Daniel didn't look back. He straightened the wheel and pushed the pedal down, and for the first time that night, he stopped caring whether the groceries arrived in one piece.

**S12 · The checkpoint** `08:39.9`

The eastern exit appeared beyond a row of dark houses. Two light towers turned the road white. Concrete barriers funneled both lanes into one. Behind a lowered gate stood a white utility van and a dark SUV. And beside the gate, a man in a white protective suit. He raised one arm. Stop. Daniel felt his chest loosen for the first time since the gas station. Someone was still working. Someone in this town still had a job, and a procedure, and a reason to be standing in the rain. Four tenths of a mile to open highway, on the other side of that gate. He stopped on the gravel turnout, well short of the barrier, and left the engine running. He even left himself room to back up. Not because he expected to need it. Habit.

**S13 · Step one** `09:28.1`

The man walked to the driver's side and stopped beside the door. White coveralls. Clear goggles. A white mask pulled tight across his mouth and nose. He lifted his hand and pointed down at the window. Driver window down. Step one. Daniel's hand was already moving toward the switch. Eleven years of habit moved it for him. Then he saw the glove. The left index finger had torn through the blue nitrile. Under it, the finger bent sideways at the middle joint, and the man didn't seem to know. Daniel took his hand off the switch. He pointed at the gate instead. The man's eyes followed his hand. Then, patiently, he pointed down at the window again. Rule four. An instruction is only as safe as the person giving it.

**S14 · The mask** `10:15.3`

Up close, the mask wasn't wet from the rain. A dark stain had spread outward from the center of it. From the inside. Above the collar of the suit, thin dark lines branched across the skin of his neck, like cracks under ice. And his eyes were wrong. Not glowing. Not red. Just clouded, like a window someone had breathed on. They weren't looking at Daniel's face. They were following his hands. Then the phone in the dash mount lit up and began to ring. Twenty-four minutes late, his dispatcher was calling back. The man's head snapped toward the sound. His hand dropped to the door handle. He pulled once. Waited. Pulled again, harder, until the whole door shook in its frame. Daniel killed the ringer. It was already too late. Behind the gate, every door of the van and the SUV stood open.

**S15 · The alarm** `11:04.9`

There was no one at the folding table under the lights. No one behind the wheel of the SUV. A second protective suit lay crumpled against the concrete barrier. Daniel had taken it for a pile of supplies. He shifted into reverse. And the truck did what fleet trucks are built to do. It began to beep. Loud. Steady. Impossible to switch off from the driver's seat. The man at the window went completely still. Behind the barrier, the crumpled suit moved. A hand came up onto the concrete. Then a head. In the open van, a third figure sat up. Every one of them turned toward the same sound. Daniel pressed the accelerator.

**S16 · One inch** `11:47.8`

The man moved with the truck. One step. Two. Then a sudden, violent burst of speed that no injured man should have had. His palm hit the glass beside Daniel's face. The mask slid down below his chin. His jaw hung open at a wrong angle, the skin of his throat pulled tight, and he made no attempt to speak. The second blow rocked the mirror on its arm. Behind him, the others were coming around the barrier, toward the beeping. The third blow didn't crack the glass. It did something worse. Somewhere inside the door, a part gave way, and the window dropped. One inch. Cold air and rain came through the gap. And with them, four gloved fingertips, curling over the top of the glass.

**S17 · The only open road** `12:34.6`

The truck was faster than they were. For a few seconds, that was enough. Daniel cut the wheel, swung the truck around on the gravel turnout, and shoved it into drive. The fingertips slid away. In the mirror, three white suits shrank into the floodlights. He pressed the window switch. The motor whined inside the door. The glass didn't move. The gap stayed open. As the truck came around, his headlights swept across a sign at the back of the checkpoint. It faced the road into town, and he had never seen its front. Temporary steel legs. A portable lamp. A white panel, printed by the county. Stop. Lower driver window for screening. It was the same sign he had passed at the town limit. Someone there had blacked out the county's instruction and written their own, in thick marker, pressed hard enough to tear. The same hand that had written no response, address after address. Someone in Bellwether had worked it out before he did. The danger had never been in the air. It was in the people asking you to let them close. The route screen recalculated. Only one road out was open. It led back through Bellwether. *[pause 4 s]*
